Make the guide work for you

How to read a background guide.

A background guide is not a textbook to memorise — it's a map of where the debate can go. Read it like a delegate, not a student.

i.

Start with the questions

Read the "questions before the floor" first. Everything else in the guide exists to help you answer them — let them direct your reading.

ii.

Locate your country in the blocs

Find where your assigned delegation actually sits. Your job is to defend a real foreign-policy position, not your personal opinion.

iii.

Follow the further-reading trail

The guide gives you the floor; the citations give you depth. The strongest delegates read two or three sources beyond the guide.

iv.

Write your position paper from it

The final section is a brief. Treat it as your assignment — it is the single best predictor of how prepared you'll feel on Day 1.

Required submission

The position paper

Every delegate submits one position paper before the conference. It states your country's stance on the topic and your proposed direction. It is reviewed by the dais and is eligible for the Best Position Paper award.

  • LengthOne to two pages (A4)
  • SectionsBackground · National stance · Proposals
  • CitationsRequired · any consistent style
  • FormatPDF, submitted via portal
  • Due28 November 2026, 23:59 SGT
  • Crisis delegatesPortfolio-specific brief

A position-paper template and worked example ship inside every background guide. First-time delegates receive light written feedback on request.
