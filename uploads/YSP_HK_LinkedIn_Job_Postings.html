What is YSP?
Youth for Sustainable Policy is a regional network of young people who research, write, and engage seriously with the policy questions shaping Southeast Asia and Greater China. We produce real policy briefs. We build real mentorship relationships. We give members real credentials — not participation certificates.

We've been building this network across ASEAN. Now we're launching in Hong Kong — because the Greater Bay Area transition, Hong Kong's role within it, and the policy literacy gap among HK youth all point to the same conclusion: this chapter needs to exist, and it needs to be built by people who actually live here.

✦ A certificate and recommendation letter from YSP Global
✦ The founding team distinction — your name attached to this chapter's origin story

The honest part:
This is a volunteer role. We are not yet funded. What we offer is real work, a real network, and a real CV line — not a salary. The commitment is 5–8 hours per week for 16 weeks, fully remote. If that trade works for you, we want to hear from you.

Leadership
About the Role

The Chapter Director is the most senior member of the YSP Hong Kong founding team. You are responsible for the chapter's overall direction, external relationships, and quality of output. You report directly to YSP Global leadership (Singapore) and serve as YSP HK's primary spokesperson with university partners, media, and institutional contacts. This is a founding role — your decisions in the first 16 weeks will define the chapter's identity for years to come.

What You'll Do
  • Lead all chapter operations and set the strategic priorities for YSP Hong Kong's first cohort
  • Own the university partnership process — initiate contact with HKU, CUHK, HKUST, PolyU, and CityU for MOU discussions
  • Chair the weekly founding team call and monthly cross-chapter coordination meeting with YSP Mainland China and Macau
  • Represent YSP HK at external events, panels, and institutional meetings
  • Oversee the Societies Ordinance (Cap. 151) registration process and manage the relationship with the Societies Officer
  • Approve all public-facing content before it is published across LinkedIn, WeChat, Rednote, and Weibo
  • Build and manage relationships with HK-based advisors, mentors, and future corporate partners
  • Produce the Chapter Director's section of the YSP HK Year 1 Impact Report
Who We're Looking For
  • Final year undergraduate or postgraduate student at a Hong Kong university, or a recent graduate (within 3 years) based in HK
  • Demonstrable interest in public policy, sustainable development, or governance — through coursework, research, or prior experience
  • Prior leadership experience in any context — student organisation, NGO, startup, or professional role
  • Comfortable communicating in both English and Traditional Chinese (Cantonese fluency a plus, not required)
  • Network within at least one HK university — a warm contact at a student union, faculty department, or policy society
  • High personal accountability and no need for micromanagement

Youth for Sustainable Policy (YSP) · Hong Kong
🌐 Remote 🕐 6–8 hrs/week 🎓 Volunteer 📍 Hong Kong preferred ⏱ 16 weeks + ongoing
Editorial
About the Role

The Head of Editorial & Research manages YSP Hong Kong's entire content pipeline — from the weekly WeChat discussion threads to the full-length policy briefs submitted to the Greater China Policy Review. You set the editorial standards, coach the three Policy Writers, and ensure that everything published under the YSP HK name is accurate, analytical, and genuinely useful. You are the chapter's chief quality controller.

What You'll Do
  • Own the monthly editorial calendar — thematic planning, assignment of policy briefs to writers, and publication scheduling across all platforms
  • Edit and provide written feedback on all policy briefs, explainers, and Rednote captions before they are approved for publication
  • Produce one original policy brief per month under your own byline as a quality benchmark for the team
  • Run the bi-weekly writers' call — 30 minutes, structured feedback, upcoming assignments, and one policy discussion topic
  • Manage the bilingual translation pipeline — ensuring Traditional Chinese and English versions of key pieces are consistent in argument and tone
  • Source and maintain the reading list for all YSP HK content — open-access sources only, with an emphasis on HK government publications, GBA policy documents, and peer-reviewed research accessible without a paywall
  • Contribute to the Greater China Policy Review quarterly publication alongside editorial leads from Mainland China and Macau chapters
Who We're Looking For
  • Strong writing ability in English — you can edit a draft and explain precisely why a sentence doesn't work
  • Reading proficiency in Traditional Chinese (writing proficiency a bonus)
  • Background in social sciences, law, economics, international relations, or a related field
  • Genuine interest in at least one of: GBA integration, climate policy, housing economics, financial regulation, or digital governance
  • Prior experience editing — a student journal, a publication, a blog, a policy body — any context counts
What You Gain

Editorial CreditNamed as Head of Editorial on all YSP HK publications — a professional editorial credential

Policy Writer (×3 Positions)
Youth for Sustainable Policy (YSP) · Hong Kong
🌐 Fully Remote 🕐 4–6 hrs/week 🎓 Volunteer 📍 Anywhere ⏱ 16 weeks
3 Spots Open
About the Role

Policy Writers are the core of what YSP produces. You research, draft, and refine one policy brief per month (800–1,200 words) and two short-form policy explainers per month (300–500 words each) for Rednote and the WeChat community. You do not need prior policy writing experience — you need the ability to read complex source material, form a clear argument, and write it in a way a non-specialist can act on. We will teach the rest.

We are recruiting three Writers with different thematic focuses so that YSP HK covers the policy landscape without overlap. Please indicate in your application which track you are applying for.

Three Writer Tracks
  • Track A — Climate & Urban Sustainability: Carbon neutrality policy, green finance, urban planning, energy transition, waste management. Relevant to HK's 2050 net-zero target and GBA environmental integration.
  • Track B — Housing, Economy & Labour: Housing affordability, talent retention policy, fintech regulation, cross-border employment, SME policy. Relevant to HK's post-2020 economic repositioning.
  • Track C — Digital Governance & Society: Data privacy, AI regulation, platform governance, digital identity, smart city policy. Relevant to HK's Smart City Blueprint and GBA data infrastructure debates.
What You'll Do
  • Produce one 800–1,200 word policy brief per month on an assigned topic within your track — researched, argued, and copy-edited to publication standard
  • Produce two 300–500 word explainers per month formatted for Rednote and WeChat — simpler language, same analytical rigour
  • Attend the bi-weekly writers' call with the Head of Editorial (30 minutes, online)
  • Post one Monday Policy Drop to the WeChat group per month in rotation with other writers
  • Submit one piece to the Greater China Policy Review at the end of the 16-week term as your capstone output
Who We're Looking For
  • Any level of study or career stage — from Year 1 undergraduates to young professionals in their first five years
  • English writing ability strong enough to produce a clear argument in 800 words — we will provide editing and feedback, but the raw material needs to be there
  • Genuine curiosity about your chosen track — not just general interest in "policy"
  • Ability to read and cite Chinese-language government sources is valued but not required
  • Based in Hong Kong or with strong knowledge of HK policy context
What You Gain
4+ BylinesMinimum four published pieces under your name over 16 weeks — concrete, shareable portfolio evidence
Editorial CoachingWritten feedback on every draft from the Head of Editorial — the equivalent of a policy writing apprenticeship
Publication CreditNamed contributor in the Greater China Policy Review, a regional YSP publication
Recommendation LetterPersonalised letter from YSP HK Chapter Director upon completion, tailored to graduate school or employment context

Reporting & Communication Structure

To YSP Global: The Chapter Director reports to YSP Global leadership (Mei Tong Kim, CEO) with a written chapter update every two weeks and attends the monthly Greater China coordination call alongside the Mainland China and Macau Chapter Directors.

Within the team: The founding team meets weekly on Zoom or Google Meet (30 minutes, strict agenda). All action items are tracked in Notion by the Community Coordinator. Every team member communicates via a shared Slack workspace and the YSP HK founding team WeChat group for mobile-first updates.

With members: Once the first cohort is recruited, the Chapter Director and Head of Editorial are the primary member-facing contacts. Policy Writers interact with members through the WeChat community group and the bi-weekly open discussion sessions. All other roles are back-of-house during the programme.
