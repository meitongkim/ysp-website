Crisis Committee Design

Realism that forces decisions.

Our crisis committees don't just simulate problems — they create cascading, interdependent crises that require delegates to track multiple developments simultaneously while staying inside formal procedure.

01

Multimedia Crisis Injections

Realistic "news wire" updates are delivered via a simulated press feed — formatted as AFP/Reuters dispatches with timestamps, source attribution, and conflicting reports. Delegates must assess credibility while debating.

02

Rapid-Response Crisis Director

The Crisis Director responds to all directives, back-channel notes, and personal action requests within 15 minutes during sessions. Crisis updates are calibrated to force debate pivots — never to ambush arbitrarily.

03

Consequence Matrix

Every directive passed by the committee has documented downstream consequences — modelled before the conference. Delegates who ignore cascade effects will face realistic counteractions from the Crisis Staff within the same session.

04

Personal Directives & Back-Channel Notes

Delegates may submit private directives to the Crisis Director via Google Forms, simulating real back-channel diplomacy. Responses are delivered in character and may influence the broader crisis arc.

05

Integrated Document Workflow

All working papers, directives, and amendments are tracked on a shared Secretariat dashboard — delegates, Chairs, and Crisis Staff can view the live state of all documents at any point in the session.

01 — Debate Flow

Mode Description Duration Type
Formal (Moderated) Caucus Delegates speak in order from the Speakers List. The Chair recognises each delegate formally. All substantive debate occurs here. Chair-set Required
Unmoderated Caucus Delegates freely negotiate, draft working papers, and form blocs. The Chair suspends formal proceedings. A motion and second are required to enter. Motion-set Required
Moderated Caucus A short-form structured discussion on a specific subtopic. Delegates motion for a moderated caucus specifying topic and speaking time (e.g., 45 seconds per speaker). Motion-set Optional
Yield to Questions A speaker may yield remaining time to questions from the floor. The Chair manages question priority. Maximum 2 questions per yield. Remaining Diplomatic
Note on Virtual Procedure: All motions are submitted via the Speakers List Google Form or typed in Zoom chat as "[DELEGATE NAME] — Motion to [action]". The Chair will verbally recognise and call the motion to the floor. This ensures procedural fidelity in the virtual setting.

05 — Diplomatic Language

Formal diplomatic register is mandatory throughout all committee sessions. First-person references ("I", "we") are prohibited. Use country-referential third person at all times.

To Agree
"The delegation of [Country] aligns with the position of the distinguished delegate…"
To Disagree
"The delegation respectfully takes issue with the assertion that…"
Yielding the Floor
"The delegation yields the remaining time to the Chair."
Requesting the Floor
"The delegation of [Country] respectfully requests to be added to the Speakers List."
Introducing a Motion
"The delegation moves for an unmoderated caucus of [X] minutes for the purpose of [Y]."
Expressing Concern
"The delegation wishes to express grave concern regarding the proposal set forth in clause [X]…"
Chair Enforcement: Chairs will issue one verbal caution for procedural language violations. A second violation results in the delegate's speaking time being suspended for the remainder of that session. Delegates are strongly encouraged to review the full Diplomatic Language Reference document provided in the delegate preparation package.

Awards & Evaluation

Substance over volume.

YSP MUN awards are determined by a structured rubric — not speaking time. Delegates who craft precision arguments, maintain foreign policy alignment, and collaborate across blocs are recognised above all others.

🏅

Best Delegate

Highest overall performance across all criteria

  • Policy Consistent, well-researched alignment with assigned country's foreign policy positions throughout all sessions
  • Writing Substantive contributions to working papers and resolutions — quality of preambulatory and operative clauses
  • Diplo. Strict adherence to formal diplomatic language and procedural correctness at all times
  • Collab. Demonstrated bloc-building, compromise-brokering, and coalition leadership
🌿

Outstanding Delegate

Strong performance with particular depth in one or two areas

  • Policy Accurate country representation with demonstrated research depth in opening statements and substantive debate
  • Writing Active contributor to working papers with clear clause-level understanding of resolution structure
  • Diplo. Consistent use of formal language with minimal procedural errors across sessions
🎙️

Verbal Commendation

Recognised for excellent diplomacy and debate skill

  • Diplo. Exceptional use of diplomatic language, persuasive rhetoric, and formal address
  • Collab. Notable contribution to cross-bloc negotiations and compromise-finding outside formal debate
📘

Best Position Paper

For the strongest pre-conference written submission

  • Policy Clear articulation of country position with cited evidence and policy framework alignment
  • Writing Well-structured analysis with proposed solutions that reflect the country's realistic policy preferences
  • Crisis For crisis committees: demonstrated anticipation of scenario developments and contingency positions

Evaluation Principles

No speaking-time bias. Delegates who speak less but more precisely are evaluated above those who speak frequently with lower quality.
Chairs do not award personal favourites. All Chair award recommendations are reviewed by the Deputy Secretary-General for consistency before announcement.
First-time delegates are evaluated within their own context. New delegates are not penalised for procedural learning curves in sessions 1–2, provided improvement is demonstrated by day three.

Global Community

Diplomacy across time zones.

Virtual conferences break geography. YSP MUN is intentionally designed to connect delegates across cultures — with structured socialising that goes beyond the committee room.

Cross-Regional Icebreakers

Structured Day 1 social activities are designed to pair delegates from different regions into small conversation groups — facilitated, not forced. Activities include regional trivia, policy debate warm-ups, and culture exchange prompts.

ASEAN-Global South Focus

YSP MUN actively recruits delegates from underrepresented regions including the Pacific Islands, Sub-Saharan Africa, and South Asia. Committee topics are chosen to give these perspectives substantive, not token, relevance in debate.

Mentorship Matching

Delegates who opt into mentorship matching during registration are paired with a more experienced delegate from a different country for a structured 45-minute pre-conference conversation and ongoing check-in support during the conference.

Post-Conference YSP Community

All MUN delegates are invited to join the wider YSP network — accessing future programmes, research routes, and the YSP alumni community across Singapore and Southeast Asia.

Multilingual Welcome

Conference communications are issued in English. Delegates who are more comfortable in Mandarin, Bahasa, or Tamil may request a pre-session language support contact from the Secretariat — no delegate should feel linguistically isolated.

Recognition Beyond Awards

Beyond formal awards, YSP MUN publishes a post-conference reflection compendium highlighting notable positions, key resolutions, and delegate insights — offering visibility to contributors who may not have taken an award home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Before you write to us.

Answers to the most common pre-conference questions. Still not sure? Email mun@ysp.org or DM us on the Discord.

Do I need prior MUN experience to apply?

No. The YSP Youth Special Assembly committee is specifically designed for first-time and newer delegates, with more structured Chair guidance and a gentler procedural learning curve. The preparation package is also designed to get any delegate conference-ready.

What software do I need to participate?

Zoom (free) and Discord (free). Both are available on all devices. A stable internet connection is strongly recommended. A laptop or desktop is preferred over mobile for committee sessions, but mobile is supported.

How are committee placements decided?

Placements are based on your committee preference ranking, experience level, and school/delegation context. The Secretariat aims to honour first-choice placements for at least 75% of delegates. Placements are final once confirmed.

Can I participate if I'm not from Singapore?

Yes — YSP MUN is a virtual conference open to youth aged 16–28 globally. The conference runs on SGT (UTC+8), and the schedule is designed to be broadly accessible from UTC-5 through UTC+9.

When are background guides released?

Background guides are released 6 weeks before the conference (by 24 October 2026) to all confirmed delegates. They are available in PDF and Google Docs format with no account required to access.

Is financial assistance genuinely available?

Yes. YSP believes no delegate should be unable to participate due to cost. Partial and full fee waivers are awarded on a needs basis. Select the relevant option in the application form; no supporting documentation is initially required.

How are crisis updates delivered during sessions?

Crisis updates are delivered via the YSP Crisis Wire — a structured Discord channel. Updates are formatted as news dispatches with timestamps. Delegates do not need to leave the Zoom session; Chairs read critical updates aloud during brief procedural pauses.

Can my school send a delegation?

Yes — group registrations of 3 or more delegates from the same school receive a reduced rate of SGD 28 per delegate. A faculty advisor contact is required for school delegations; advisors may attend as observers at no cost.
